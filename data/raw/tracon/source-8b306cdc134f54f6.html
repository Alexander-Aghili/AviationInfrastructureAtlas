Subject: WARNING FOR THE POSSIBILTY OF GLIDER ACTIVITY IN THE VICINITY OF HILLSBORO, OREGON AND THE NEWBERG VOR

Cancellation: 04/12/2023 1200 (UTC)

Glider Activity in the vicinity of Hillsboro (HIO) Airport is often a challenge for Air Traffic Control due to RADAR limitations and lack of direct communications with the Glider.

A particular concern is the BERNI, CHISM and FARMINGTON Standard Instrument Departure (SID) procedure out of HIO Airport. HETAT waypoint, on the BERNI and CHISM SIDs, is approximately 4 nautical miles west of HIO.  In reference to the ground, HETAT is near the intersection of Susbauer and Wren Roads. This area includes a large cluster of greenhouses, which provide thermal lift desirable to the glider community.  On occasion, this thermal lift can carry a glider as high as 10,000 feet, making this a popular location.  Another popular glider location is the area often referred to as the “Y”.  The “Y” is the junction of Highways 26 and 6, which provides lift from the concrete and asphalt that make up the interchange.

HIO Instrument Flight Rules (IFR) departure aircraft often utilize these SIDs. These procedures include a sweeping left turn to the south after departure from Runway 31L.  During this left turn, aircraft are “belly up” to the greenhouses and the “Y” intersection.  In addition, flight crews are looking into the sun during late afternoon departures.  This makes for a difficult environment to observe gliders that may be operating in tight circles while tracking thermals upward.  In past Near Mid-Air Collision (NMAC) reports, IFR flight crews indicated they did not see the glider until it was too late to take evasive action. 

North Plains Gliderport is depicted on the Seattle Sectional Chart and is located northwest of HIO.  Glider pilots are aware of and comply with all FAA regulations regarding airspace use.  Glider activity is dependent upon prevailing weather conditions and operate under Visual Flight Rules (VFR).  Notifications to local aviators in the form of NOTAMS are not issued when gliders are operating.  Glider activity is mostly invisible to Air Traffic Control radar, if the glider pilot is not using an operating transponder on code 1202.  

Currently, HIO Tower receives notification from the Willamette Valley Soaring Club, when glider operations commence for the day at the North Plains Gliderport. HIO Tower then includes a statement on the HIO ATIS denoting glider activity in the area.  When you hear this statement on the HIO ATIS, pilots are encouraged to maintain extra vigilance for glider operations and be familiar with popular glider areas.  Gliders are also encouraged to utilize transponders, be familiar with HIO IFR and VFR traffic patterns, and monitor PDX approach control frequencies.  It is incumbent upon both the glider pilot and fixed wing pilot to maintain extra vigilance when transiting these areas during visual meteorological conditions.

Note: Although this LTA specifically addresses HIO area, gliders can be encountered anywhere in the Willamette Valley, including MMV and Newberg areas.
